## AIM (Analytics and Insights Matter)

**What AIM is.** A analytics, applied-research, and program-evaluation firm. Primary contact: Randy. AIM combines advanced analytics, social science, and business-performance insight (customer and employee experience, driver and financial-linkage modeling, voice-of-customer, and program/impact evaluation).

**The partnership.** Semantic Arts connects a client's fragmented customer data into a reusable ontology and knowledge graph; AIM runs the driver, threshold, financial-linkage, and voice-of-customer analysis on that connected foundation; together they deliver a prioritized action plan. The client keeps the reusable data foundation, so each later question costs less than the last (no lock-in).

**Working offer name:** **The Connected Customer** (customer-experience / Customer 360 use case).

**Ideal customer profile.** Large, customer-intensive enterprises that are "data rich, insights poor," with a real CX mandate and customer data fragmented across survey, contact-center, CRM, transactional, and social systems. Best-fit sectors: financial services and insurance, healthcare, telecom, travel, retail, and other large B2B/B2C enterprises. (Restaurant chains are not an ideal Semantic Arts client and are de-emphasized.) Primary buyers: CCO, VP Customer Experience, VP Customer Insights; technical champion: CDO / VP Data & Analytics.

**Positioning against "we do analytics in house."** The gap is usually qualitative, not quantitative: strong in-house analysts are rarely trained to translate a model into the big-picture business case. AIM's craft is that translation, plus specialized causal and financial-linkage modeling and capacity for a focused, time-boxed study. Semantic Arts leaves a governed, reusable graph, so it is capability, not dependency.

**Differentiators for larger clients (AIM as an adjunct to in-house analytics).** AIM's core market is not large enterprises, but paired with Semantic Arts it is a strong complement to a Fortune 500 in-house analytics team, not a replacement:
- **Independence.** An outside view, free of internal history and politics, to choose the data, methods, and techniques a situation actually needs and to pursue ideas internal teams may not.
- **Methodological depth.** Corporate analytics teams are strong on BI, dashboards, and machine learning but rarely trained in survey measurement and causal identification. AIM brings experimental and survey design that gets at the "why" behind the metrics.
- **Augmentation.** Complements internal capabilities with specialized methods, absorbs overflow work, and validates the internal team's results.
- **Speed.** Internal teams often carry a multi-month backlog, so a new request goes to the end of the queue. AIM can turn around high-priority, short-timeframe work with hands-on PhD-level expertise.
- **Data-ontology context.** AIM analyzes with a deep understanding of the ontology co-created with Semantic Arts, which sharpens the "why" behind the data.

**Proof point.** AIM's 20+ years of driver modeling for consumer brands (e.g., the Five Guys relationship: CSAT key-driver and employee "brand champion" causal models over a 15+ year engagement). Frame results as AIM's stated outcomes; confirm figures before external use.

**Pharma / clinical-trial offering (separate campaign).** A joint drug-discovery and clinical-trial-performance offering focused on patient recruitment and retention. Problems addressed: sites that cannot enroll, sites overburdened to the point of patient fatigue and staff burnout, and unexplained attrition. Split: Semantic Arts understands and updates the data ontology and architecture; AIM enriches the data, analyzes it, produces insights and actions, and tracks and adjusts. Target clinical-operations, feasibility/site-management, and clinical-data leaders at pharma companies and CROs.
