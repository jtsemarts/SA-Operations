## Beliefs

We have some shared beliefs that are non-mainstream, but our commitment to them is continually reinforced in our work with clients and internally.

- Even the largest organization has an implicit, simple semantic data model (an enterprise ontology) at its core.
- Uncovering and elaborating that model has widespread and systemic economic advantages to the enterprise.
- Nearly half of a large company's IT budget is spent on building and maintaining systems integrations, and yet most data landscapes are still woefully disintegrated.
- The cost of this integration debt may be more acutely felt outside the IT department, where armies of analysts waste person-centuries on extraction and reconciliation of data pulled from myriad sources.
- The safest path to digital transformation is through a modular, data-first approach.
- Intractable legacy systems can be "modernized" at a fraction of the going price, again by starting with the data.
- Strict adherence to open standards is the best preventive to vendor lock-in.
- The directed graph is a more powerful and flexible basis for enterprise databases.
- The most flexible and stable standards over the last 20 years have been RDF, SPARQL, and OWL.
- Most typical enterprise application functionality can be replaced with no-code and low-code implementations.
- Most of the content in documents and other unstructured sources is about concepts that are in the semantic core model. Enterprises don't keep documents that don't relate to the core mission.
- The most productive route to integrating structured and unstructured data runs through the enterprise ontology.

## Vision

A world where enterprise information is widely understood and easily accessed by all who have permission. Most companies solve their information-systems problems by implementing application systems (payroll, inventory, customer relations, and so on). By the time firms get very large, they have thousands of these application systems, each its own data silo, and putting Humpty Dumpty back together consumes most of the IT resources of most medium-to-large firms. Semantic Arts helps clients reverse this process by focusing first on designing a single, simple core data model, then helping clients migrate data and functionality to that core.

## Mission

To lead as many firms as possible on their journey to a data-centric future. Since 2000 we have focused singularly on this mission. We have assembled what may be the largest team in the world of professionals focused solely on it, and have built the methodology, tools, and quality-assurance processes to keep delivering the highest possible quality outcomes.

## Values

- **Create Value:** all our projects should create value for clients. We do not set up situations where clients pay us because we have coerced them or made them dependent on us.
- **One Voice:** we can disagree in the office but present our best consensus when we are with the client.
- **Share Equitably:** we look for ways to share the results of our efforts with clients, partners, and employees. Equitable sharing is based on contribution.
- **Lifetime Learning:** we value continual study and research to find better ways and stay current, rather than milking whatever expertise we have.
- **Show Respect:** while focusing on results, we stay mindful of the contribution, options, and rights of others, and remain humble in all our dealings.
- **Have Fun:** we are not here to suffer for the sake of the firm. We can accomplish all we set out to, stay true to our values, have a good time, and take time off to enjoy our outside lives.

## Employee-governed

As of 2025, Semantic Arts is an employee-governed company. It has most of the trappings of an employee-owned firm, with one important exception: the employees cannot sell the company.

In 2025, ownership transferred to an irrevocable trust. The trust owns all the equity and intellectual property of the firm, and Semantic Arts pays a royalty to the trust for the right of self-determination. (Note to private equity firms: not only are we not for sale, we cannot be sold.)

Implications that shape how the firm operates:

- **Profitability is an obligation, not a maximization target.** The company must stay profitable to stay in business (there is no transferable equity and no outside investment, and a line of credit covers short-term working capital, but it cannot run multi-year deficits). This is very different from maximizing profit.
- **Fair, stable rates.** Because we are not maximizing profit, we set a fair rate and maintain it, and all clients are treated equally.
- **We do not squeeze employees.** We pay employees for all the time they work and share the value created from their contribution, avoiding the burnout model of extracting "free" chargeable work.
- **We do not take advantage of partners.** We operate under an ethically based set of principles for cooperating with partners, rather than winning their help and then excluding them or squeezing their margins.
- **Shared mission and aligned incentives** across a large number of shareholder-employees.

## Goals (in ranked priority order)

The goals below are listed in priority order, highest first. Each becomes the focus only once the goals above it are satisfied. Maximizing employee and owner pay is intentionally last: it is the next goal in line once everything above it is met, which is why the firm optimizes for survival, mission, growth, and people before pay maximization.

1. Survive.
2. Make a difference in the world.
3. Thrive and grow.
4. Continue to push the envelope of knowledge.
5. Provide an environment to foster individual growth.
6. Give staff a feeling of purpose.
7. Provide a competitive wage.
8. Optimize stress (not too much, not too little).
9. Remove toxic people (the "no assholes" rule).
10. Maximize employee pay.
11. Maximize owner's pay.

*When goals appear to conflict, the higher-ranked goal wins.*
