**Maintained by:** Brand · **Last updated:** July 3, 2026 · Living document

## 1. Overview & why it matters for Semantic Arts

Semantic Arts speaks to a senior, technical audience that values precision and dislikes filler. How the firm writes shapes whether those buyers trust what it knows. This knowledge base captures current thinking on B2B voice, plain language, and thought-leadership credibility.

## 2. Current landscape & key trends (2025-2026)

Research on B2B buyers finds that a large majority of decision-makers judge a company more by its thought-leadership content than by its marketing materials ([TopRank](https://www.toprankmarketing.com/blog/b2b-thought-leadership-2026/)). Credibility increasingly rests on proof: original research and third-party data separate insight from opinion ([Bluetext](https://bluetext.com/blog/building-trust-through-authentic-b2b-marketing-campaigns/)).

Voice-and-tone practice has matured into simple, testable frameworks. The Nielsen Norman Group's four dimensions (formal vs. casual, serious vs. funny, respectful vs. irreverent, matter-of-fact vs. enthusiastic) give a manageable way to define and hold a voice, with voice constant and tone flexing by context ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tone-of-voice-dimensions/)). Plain-language guidance is consistent across sources: short sentences, common words, and organizing for the reader raise comprehension and trust ([Digital.gov](https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language), [Coforma](https://coforma.io/perspectives/use-plain-language-to-build-trust)). Style guides built from real examples cut revision rounds and speed reviews ([HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/blog/tabid/6307/bid/31247/the-simple-template-for-a-thorough-content-style-guide.aspx)).

## 3. Best practices

Keep one voice and let tone flex by channel. Write plainly and substantiate every claim with a reason, example, or source. Build the style guide and voice profile from approved samples, not abstract rules. Use "this, not that" examples. Keep terminology consistent (an ontology firm should write "gist" the same way every time).

## 4. Application to Semantic Arts

SA has a real advantage: its data-centric thesis is now being echoed by BCG, EY, and Forrester, which is strong third-party proof for its positioning. Brand should convert that into buyer-facing credibility. The firm's own rules (no em dashes, plain language, substantiation) fit a technical audience well. Brand maintains the living style guide and voice profile in this folder, updated only from approved samples, and reviews outward-facing work for voice.

## 5. Recommendations / opportunities

Grow the style guide and voice profile from real approved pieces. Build a proof/market-validation library (the BCG/EY/Forrester pieces) that Sales and Research can reuse. Standardize a review checklist (voice, plainness, evidence). Position SA as the firm that has been doing data-centric work since before the big consultancies discovered it.
