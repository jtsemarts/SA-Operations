# Executive Salary Surveys — President, COO, CEO

**Prepared for:** JT Metcalf, COO, Semantic Arts
**Prepared by:** Otto
**Date:** June 29, 2026

---

## Benchmark Parameters

| Parameter | Setting |
|---|---|
| Roles surveyed | President, COO, CEO (3 separate surveys) |
| Geography | National (US) |
| Compensation scope | **Base + annual bonus = total cash** (equity / long-term incentives excluded) |
| Firm size | ~30 employees |
| Firm revenue | $5M–$10M |
| Firm type | Privately held, specialist professional-services firm |
| Peer framings | (A) Specialist consulting / professional services, and (B) Technology / data services — shown side by side |
| Percentiles | 25th / 50th (median) / 75th |

All figures are **annual USD, total cash (base + target bonus)** unless noted.

---

## Methodology & How to Read This

These surveys **triangulate** publicly available 2025–2026 compensation aggregator data (SalaryCube size-banded executive tables, ZipRecruiter, PayScale, and private-company survey commentary from BDO and Gallagher) and scale it to a ~30-person, $5–10M-revenue private firm. Public exec-comp data thins out at this size, so the ranges below are **directional estimates**, not a statistically sampled survey.

Two peer framings are shown for each role:

- **Framing A — Specialist consulting / professional services.** Privately held, bootstrapped/owner-operated firms in this segment tend toward conservative cash and lighter formal bonus structures. This is the closest match to Semantic Arts.
- **Framing B — Technology / data services.** Treating the firm as a tech/data-services company (knowledge-graph / ontology / software-adjacent) pulls cash compensation upward roughly 10–25% and weights bonus more heavily.

The true market for Semantic Arts most likely sits **between A and B, closer to A**.

> **Recommendation:** For any board- or compensation-committee-grade decision, validate these figures against one purchased survey (e.g., Croner, Empsight, Gallagher, or an industry-association professional-services survey). These estimates are well-suited for internal planning and sanity-checking, not for sole reliance in a formal pay action.

## Survey 1 — President

**Role context:** Leads the consulting/delivery side of the firm and owns client deliverables; high responsibility for budgets and headcount; 5–10 years tenure at the firm but **under one year in the President role**. *Surveyed as a pure executive role — this excludes any billable/consulting compensation she earns personally.*

Because the President owns the revenue-generating delivery organization, the role benchmarks **above the COO and just below the CEO** in a firm of this size.

### Framing A — Specialist consulting / professional services

| Component | 25th | 50th (median) | 75th |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base salary | $195K | $240K | $295K |
| Target bonus | 15% | 22% | 30% |
| **Total cash** | **$220K** | **$285K** | **$370K** |

### Framing B — Technology / data services

| Component | 25th | 50th (median) | 75th |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base salary | $225K | $280K | $340K |
| Target bonus | 20% | 28% | 35% |
| **Total cash** | **$260K** | **$340K** | **$440K** |

**Calibration note:** Under a year in the role argues for positioning toward the **25th–50th percentile** today (typical for someone newly elevated), with a defined path to median-and-above as she establishes the role and delivers against budget/headcount targets. The blended (A↔B) midpoint for the role lands near **$300K–$315K total cash**.

## Survey 2 — COO

**Role context:** Leads operations across HR, marketing, sales, and much of finance plus general office management; two direct reports; broad "operational generalist" scope typical of a COO at a sub-50-person firm.

### Framing A — Specialist consulting / professional services

| Component | 25th | 50th (median) | 75th |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base salary | $165K | $205K | $250K |
| Target bonus | 10% | 18% | 25% |
| **Total cash** | **$185K** | **$235K** | **$300K** |

### Framing B — Technology / data services

| Component | 25th | 50th (median) | 75th |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base salary | $190K | $235K | $285K |
| Target bonus | 15% | 22% | 30% |
| **Total cash** | **$215K** | **$280K** | **$360K** |

**Calibration note:** Public data for COOs at 1–50-employee firms clusters at **$180K–$260K base / $225K–$350K total cash**, which both framings sit within. Internal-equity rule of thumb: COO total cash typically runs **60–85% of CEO total cash**. The blended midpoint lands near **$255K total cash**.

## Survey 3 — CEO

**Role context:** Top executive of a ~30-person, $5–10M-revenue privately held firm. At this size the CEO/owner role carries full enterprise accountability but modest absolute cash relative to mid-market peers.

### Framing A — Specialist consulting / professional services

| Component | 25th | 50th (median) | 75th |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base salary | $215K | $265K | $330K |
| Target bonus | 15% | 22% | 30% |
| **Total cash** | **$240K** | **$310K** | **$410K** |

### Framing B — Technology / data services

| Component | 25th | 50th (median) | 75th |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base salary | $250K | $310K | $385K |
| Target bonus | 20% | 28% | 40% |
| **Total cash** | **$290K** | **$370K** | **$490K** |

**Calibration note:** Aggregator data for CEOs of firms in the **$5M–$25M** revenue band shows base $225K–$350K and total cash $250K–$450K; the **under-$50M** band tops out near $500K total cash (driven by the larger end of that band). A $5–10M firm should sit in the lower-to-middle of those ranges. The blended midpoint lands near **$340K total cash**.

## Internal-Equity Snapshot (median, blended A↔B)

| Role | Approx. median total cash | Relationship |
|---|---|---|
| CEO | ~$340K | Top of structure |
| President | ~$310K | ~90% of CEO — runs delivery / revenue engine |
| COO | ~$255K | ~75% of CEO — operations & shared services |

This ordering (CEO > President > COO) is consistent with typical professional-services hierarchies where the President owns client delivery and the COO owns internal operations. Adjust if scope or P&L ownership differs from the assumptions above.

---

## Key Caveats

- Figures **exclude equity, profit-share, and long-term incentives** per the requested scope. At privately held firms of this size, those components are often where owners/executives capture the most value, so total *economic* compensation can sit well above the cash figures here.
- These are **national** figures. Applying a Colorado / Northern-Colorado cost-of-labor adjustment would modestly lower the national medians (Fort Collins typically runs slightly below the national average for executive roles).
- Ranges are directional triangulations from public aggregators, not a proprietary sampled survey. Treat them as planning guidance.
