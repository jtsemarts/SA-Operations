## Why the First Pass Ran High

Your read is correct. The first surveys leaned on commercial pay aggregators (SalaryCube, Cowen Partners, BDO private-company data). Those sources skew high for a firm like Semantic Arts for three structural reasons:

1. **Sample bias toward larger and funded firms.** Their "C-suite" samples are dominated by VC/PE-backed and larger private companies. Even their "small company" bands often top out at $25M–$50M revenue — 3–5x our size.
2. **Target market comp, not realized pay.** They report what firms *aim* to offer to compete for talent, not what executives at small firms actually draw.
3. **They don't isolate overhead roles.** A President/COO/CEO who is non-billable overhead at a 30-person professional-services firm is a different cost center than a revenue-driving executive at a scaling tech company.

When you anchor instead to **actual paid wages** (BLS) and to **what a $5–10M firm can afford**, the defensible numbers come down materially.

---

## Anchor 1 — BLS Actual-Wage Data (the strongest evidence)

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics OEWS program reports *actual wages paid*, across all employer sizes, from a mandatory employer survey — not aspirational market targets. For **Chief Executives (SOC 11-1011), May 2023, national:**

| Statistic | Annual wage |
|---|---|
| 10th percentile | $80,000 |
| 25th percentile | $130,840 |
| **50th percentile (median)** | **$206,680** |
| Mean | $258,900 |
| 75th / 90th percentile | Not published (BLS caps reporting at ≥$239,200/yr) |

Industry cut — **Management, Scientific & Technical Consulting Services (NAICS 541600)**, the closest industry match to Semantic Arts: Chief Executive **mean** wage **$274,920**. Note this is a *mean* (pulled upward by large consultancies), against an all-industry **median** of **$206,680**.

For the **President / COO** roles, the relevant lower anchor is **General & Operations Managers (SOC 11-1021)**, whose national median wage is roughly **$100K** (a broad pool including mid-level managers). The true executive figure for a small firm sits **between** the G&OM median (~$100K) and the Chief Executive median (~$207K).

**Takeaway:** The actual-wage median for a U.S. chief executive is ~$207K — well below the ~$340K aggregator "market" median from the first pass. For a sub-$10M overhead role, the median-to-mean band of roughly **$200K–$275K** is the realistic top-of-house anchor.

## Anchor 2 — Affordability at $5–10M Revenue

Owner/executive compensation as a share of revenue declines sharply with firm size. Published professional-services benchmarks (total comp, salary + distributions):

| Revenue | Top-executive comp as % of revenue |
|---|---|
| $1–3M | 8–15% |
| **$5M** | **5–8%** |
| **$10M** | **4–6%** |
| $25M | 3–4% |
| $50M | 2–3% |

Applied to Semantic Arts (midpoint ~$7.5M revenue), and remembering these are **three overhead executives drawing salary**:

- A grounded scenario (CEO ~$245K + President ~$225K + COO ~$200K = **~$670K**) is **~9% of $7.5M revenue** for the three roles combined.
- The first-pass aggregator scenario (~$905K combined) would be **~12% of revenue** — and over 18% at the $5M end.

Since consulting operating margins typically run 15–30%, every point of overhead-exec comp comes directly out of margin. This math is itself an argument for discipline: the aggregator medians are affordable only by consuming an outsized share of margin.

## Revised, Defensible Position (Total Cash = Base + Bonus, National)

Anchored to BLS actual wages and small-firm affordability, positioning these as **overhead executive roles at a privately held, sub-$10M professional-services firm**:

| Role | 25th | 50th (median) | 75th |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CEO** | $195K | **$245K** | $310K |
| **President** | $180K | **$225K** | $285K |
| **COO** | $160K | **$200K** | $255K |

Hierarchy preserved (CEO > President > COO), with the top role landing near the BLS chief-executive median/mean band and the other two scaled below as overhead functions.

### How this compares to the first pass

| Role | First pass (aggregator) median | Grounded median | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| CEO | ~$340K | ~$245K | −$95K (~28% lower) |
| President | ~$310K | ~$225K | −$85K (~27% lower) |
| COO | ~$255K | ~$200K | −$55K (~22% lower) |

## Practical Guidance

- **For setting overhead-exec pay at a firm this size, the grounded table is the defensible reference.** The aggregator figures are useful only as an *upper bound* — what you'd need to pay to pull an executive away from a larger or funded employer.
- **Owner distributions are separate.** If the CEO/President is an owner, profit distributions sit on top of (and are analyzed separately from) the salary benchmarks here. Keep them out of the wage comparison to avoid double-counting.
- **The President's tenure (<1 year in role)** argues for the 25th–50th percentile of the grounded range today.
- **Colorado / Fort Collins** would pull these national figures down slightly further (Fort Collins runs below the Denver metro and the national average for executive roles).
- **Caveats:** BLS data is May 2023 (most recent finalized occupation detail); apply ~3–5% for wage growth to 2026. BLS caps chief-executive reporting at $239,200, so it understates the genuine top tail — which is fine here, since we are positioning overhead roles, not the top tail.
