# HR — Knowledge Base: Small Professional-Services HR & Compliance

**Maintained by:** HR · **Last updated:** July 3, 2026 · Living document · *HR best-practice guidance, not legal advice.*

## 1. Overview & why it matters for Semantic Arts

A ~30-person firm hiring across many states behaves like a small multi-state employer with an outsized compliance surface. This knowledge base captures the HR landscape SA operates in: compensation, benefits, recruiting, and the compliance areas that carry the most risk.

## 2. Current landscape & key trends (2025–2026)

**Pay transparency** keeps spreading — roughly 18+ states plus DC now require pay disclosure; a practical multi-state approach is to standardize on the strictest applicable rule. Colorado (home state) is among the strictest: postings must include a pay range, benefits description, and application window ([Paycor](https://www.paycor.com/resource-center/articles/pay-transparency-laws-by-state/), [Colorado EPEWA](https://cdle.colorado.gov/dlss/labor-laws-by-topic/equal-pay-for-equal-work-act)).

**FLSA exemption** is unsettled: the 2024 rule raising the exempt salary threshold was vacated, returning the federal floor to $35,568 while an appeal continues — classification calls are high-risk ([DOL](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/overtime/salary-levels)).

**I-9/work authorization:** current form (edition 01/20/2025); the remote document-examination alternative is available only to employers enrolled and in good standing with E-Verify ([USCIS](https://www.uscis.gov/i-9)).

**Compensation practice:** benchmark against blended, reputable sources rather than one feed, anchor to a stated philosophy (target percentile, band width), and review at least annually. **PTO/leave** rules vary sharply by state — several states ban use-it-or-lose-it and require payout of accrued vacation (California, Colorado, and others), which is part of why unlimited-PTO policies must be carefully drafted.

## 3. Best practices

Post pay ranges by default across the multi-state footprint. Treat classification (exempt/non-exempt, employee/contractor) as high-risk and route borderline calls to Legal/counsel. Keep the I-9 separate from the personnel file and follow the retention schedule. Benchmark comp from multiple sources with an explicit philosophy. Build from standard templates; flag missing ones rather than improvising.

## 4. Application to Semantic Arts

SA's multi-state hiring, S-corp structure, and competitive benefits (unlimited PTO, low-cost insurance, device-purchase assistance, retirement match, training budget) shape the work. The consultant pay model (incentivized hourly, paid OT) reads as non-exempt — worth stating explicitly on offers. HR executes the HR-function SOPs on command (offers, onboarding, comp reviews) and flags gaps through Otto; it defers legal determinations to Legal.

**Payroll is non-standard:** it runs **every 4 weeks (13 periods/year), not monthly**, and the **workweek runs Thursday to Friday** (a week ends Thursday midnight, starts Friday morning), so pay periods start on a Friday and end on a Thursday. Use the dated "2026 pay periods" schedule in the Company Calendar for exact process and pay dates; never assume monthly payroll or a Monday-start week.

## 5. Recommendations / opportunities

Keep a state-by-state employment matrix (with Legal). Default to pay-transparency-compliant postings. Confirm FLSA classifications and state them on offers. Run the annual comp review from blended benchmarks. Keep templates current (offer, onboarding, personnel file already captured). Watch the FLSA appeal and pay-transparency expansion.
