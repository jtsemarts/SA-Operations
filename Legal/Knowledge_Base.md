# Legal — Knowledge Base: Small Multi-State Firm Law

**Maintained by:** Legal · **Last updated:** July 3, 2026 · Living document · *General information, not legal advice.*

## 1. Overview & why it matters for Semantic Arts

Semantic Arts earns revenue across nearly all US states and works with some overseas clients, so it carries a multi-state and cross-border legal surface far larger than its ~30-person headcount implies. This knowledge base captures the areas that recur and where the law is currently in flux.

## 2. Current landscape & key trends (2025–2026)

**Non-competes:** the FTC's 2024 nationwide ban was vacated; enforcement reverted to case-by-case, so non-compete questions now turn on **state law**, which ranges from California's near-total prohibition to states enforcing reasonable restraints ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/federal-trade-commission-files-accede-vacatur-non-compete-clause-rule)).

**Worker classification** remains treacherous: a person can be a valid contractor under the federal "economic reality" test yet an employee under a state ABC test ([DOL](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/flsa/misclassification/rulemaking/faqs)).

**State tax nexus:** post-*Wayfair* economic nexus (often ~$100K thresholds, with states dropping transaction-count tests). Simply having clients in a state usually does not by itself require foreign qualification — a physical presence, in-state employee, or property is the more typical trigger ([Sales Tax Institute](https://www.salestaxinstitute.com/resources/economic-nexus-state-guide), [Wolters Kluwer](https://www.wolterskluwer.com/en/expert-insights/doing-business-in-another-state-foreign-qualification)).

**Cross-border data:** for EU/UK personal data, the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework (valid but under challenge) and Standard Contractual Clauses (with the UK addendum) are the mechanisms, and transfers require a risk assessment ([European Commission SCCs](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/international-dimension-data-protection/standard-contractual-clauses-scc_en)).

**Contracts:** the MSA + SOW structure dominates services work; the clauses that most affect risk are limitation of liability, IP/work-product ownership vs. license, indemnification, and termination.

## 3. Best practices

Ask which jurisdiction governs before analyzing anything. Spot issues early and route consequential questions to counsel — for a firm this size, fractional or matter-specific outside counsel is usually right-sized. Cite authority and note currency. Keep the I-9 and personnel records per the retention schedule. Treat everything as confidential.

## 4. Application to Semantic Arts

The firm's footprint means employment-law variance (PTO/leave, non-compete, classification), multi-state registration/nexus, and cross-border data terms are recurring. Legal is draft-only and works from what is provided; its job is issue-spotting and boundary-flagging, coordinating with HR (employment) and pointing to counsel for determinations. The Canada/UK subsidiary work adds international employment and data considerations.

## 5. Recommendations / opportunities

Maintain a simple state-by-state matrix of where SA has employees and the obligations that attach. Standardize contract review against the high-risk clauses above. Confirm a DPA/SCC posture for overseas clients. Keep a short "when to call counsel" trigger list. Re-verify volatile areas (non-compete, classification, pay transparency) against current sources rather than assuming.

## Firm AI Usage Policy (a standing compliance duty)

Semantic Arts adopted an AI Usage Policy (v1.1, effective June 7, 2026; owner: Rebecca Younes, President). Legal folds this into its knowledge base and screens every proposed agent action against it, flagging anything that could violate it to Otto for the COO's review before it proceeds. The policy and Legal's flagging checklist and build-compliance review are kept in `Agents/Legal/AI_Usage_Policy_Compliance.md` (source PDF in `Agents/_Shared/`). Key rules: company-managed Claude/ChatGPT only for client data, IP, and deliverables (never personal or non-company accounts); minimize and, where possible, anonymize sensitive and highly sensitive data; do not use AI to design ontologies/schemas/models or make business decisions; keep AI in "propose, don't execute" mode with human oversight of actions; connect to company systems only via company-managed, company-approved integrations; never store credentials in AI-accessible locations; and disclose AI use in client work. Our current build is strongly aligned (company account, connector-free, draft-only); the open items are the stored service-account key and the personal-app (Faithful Steward) integration, both flagged in the compliance file.
